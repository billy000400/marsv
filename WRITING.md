# WRITING.md — high-priority report rules

These rules apply to every `REPORT*.md`, including follow-ups and supplements.

1. Write in plain language for a new researcher.
2. State the concrete research question before presenting methods.
3. Define every necessary technical term when it first appears.
4. Do not invent labels such as “basin geometry,” “walls,” or “dose–response”
   unless they are necessary and explicitly defined.
5. Do not add unrequested statistical tests or analyses unless they are needed
   to answer the research question; if added, explain why.
6. Clearly distinguish observation, correlation, hypothesis, and causal mechanism.
7. Never present correlation from one checkpoint as evidence of a training mechanism.
8. Prefer direct plots and measurements over unnecessary statistical formalism.
9. Every conclusion must name exactly which result, figure, table, or experiment supports it.
10. Match the requested research question and output path exactly; do not substitute a
    nearby question or a conventional filename.
